title: Responsible use
description: The VeriWorkly ATS Engine is for candidates checking their own resume, not for screening or ranking applicants. Why, and what to tell your users.
---

The VeriWorkly ATS Engine is a tool for candidates who want to see their own resume as software sees it. It is not built or validated for decisions about other people.

<Callout type="warn" title="Intended use">
  Using it to screen, rank or reject applicants may make it an automated employment decision tool
  under laws such as New York City Local Law 144, the EU AI Act (which treats recruitment as high
  risk), Illinois HB 3773 and Colorado SB 26-189. Those laws carry bias-audit, notice and
  documentation duties that this package does not meet for you.
</Callout>

## What a score means

A score measures how well a resume parses and reads. It says nothing about the person. The rubric tests whether an ATS can find a name, a contact line, dated roles and standard headings, and whether the layout survives extraction. It does not test ability, fit or potential.

## Why it is not for screening

- **No validation for hiring.** The rubric was built to explain how a document reads. It has not been validated for decisions about other people.
- **Format is not merit.** Layout, language and convention differ by country, field and career stage. A candidate with a perfectly good resume in a layout the parser reads badly would be ranked down for the wrong reason.
- **No audit.** Laws that cover automated employment decisions ask for bias audits, notices to candidates and records. The engine does none of that for you.
- **No ranking.** The engine scores one resume at a time. Comparing candidates by score is the use the intended-use notice rules out, and it is not something the engine plans to support. See the [roadmap](/ats-engine/project/roadmap).

## What a host should tell users

If you build a product on the engine, say these things where the score is shown:

1. The score is about how software reads the document. It is not a judgement of the person.
2. The score comes from a published, deterministic rubric. Link to the [rubric](/ats-engine/reference/rubric) so a user can see every rule.
3. The engine does not simulate any ATS vendor. Where the report shows a named ATS's notes, they are what the vendor documents on its own public pages, each with its source.
4. Resume text goes where your product sends it. Say whether it stays on your server, and whether any of it goes to a model provider (see [Security](/ats-engine/project/security)).
5. The score is for the candidate. Do not show it to a recruiter as a judgement of an applicant, and do not use it to filter or order applicants.

## AI output is the model's words

The optional `/ai` tasks run on your own API key and never change a score. A model's explanation or recommendation is not grounded in the resume and is not a fact about the candidate. Label it as the model's words wherever you show it. Identity values a model returns (names, employers, titles, schools, emails, URLs, skills) are checked against the resume text and dropped if they are not there, and `parsed.provenance` records which fields a model recovered. See the [AI guide](/ats-engine/guides/ai).

## Restricting what a client sees

`shapeReport(report, "restricted")` trims a report before you send it to a client: scores, verdict and the top fix, with counts in place of keyword and role lists. See [Restricted reports](/ats-engine/guides/restricted-reports).
